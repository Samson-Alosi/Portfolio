Looking after your mind doesn't need a big life overhaul. Often it comes down to small, repeatable habits. The [NHS's five steps to mental wellbeing](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/five-steps-to-mental-wellbeing) are a good starting point. They are connecting with others, being active, learning new skills, giving to others, and paying attention to the present moment. Here are five practical tips, with the research behind each so you can check it yourself.

### 1. Invest in your relationships

If you only change one thing, make it this. The [Harvard Study of Adult Development](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2023/02/work-out-daily-ok-but-how-socially-fit-are-you) has followed people since 1938 and is among the longest studies of adult life ever run. Its director says the quality of our connections strongly affects both our health and our happiness. Researchers also found that [satisfaction with relationships at age 50 predicted health at 80](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2017/04/over-nearly-80-years-harvard-study-has-been-showing-how-to-live-a-healthy-and-happy-life/) better than cholesterol levels did.

**Try this:** Message one person today, just to check in. Then put a regular call or coffee in the diary. Quality matters more than quantity, so a few warm, reliable relationships beat a large but shallow circle.

### 2. Move your body, in a way you'll keep doing

Exercise isn't only for physical fitness. According to the NHS, being active can [raise self-esteem and help you set and achieve goals](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stress-anxiety-depression/improve-mental-wellbeing/), and you don't need to spend hours in a gym.

The research on depression is encouraging, though it comes with caveats. A [Cochrane review](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD004366_exercise-treatment-depression) of 73 studies found that exercise may reduce depressive symptoms compared with no treatment. It also found probably little to no difference compared with psychological therapy, and possibly none compared with antidepressants. The authors point out that the quality of many studies was low and long-term evidence is limited. A large [BMJ analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10870815/) found that walking or jogging, yoga and strength training were among the most effective forms, and that yoga and strength training were well tolerated.

**Try this:** Pick something you enjoy, such as a brisk 20-minute walk, a yoga video or a simple strength routine, and schedule it like an appointment. Exercise supports treatment for depression. It doesn't replace professional care.

### 3. Protect your sleep

Sleep and mental health affect each other in both directions. The [Mental Health Foundation](https://www.mentalhealth.org.uk/publications/how-sleep-better) explains that people with anxiety or depression are more likely to struggle with sleep, and that poor sleep can make those symptoms worse. Its report [*Taking Sleep Seriously*](https://www.mentalhealth.org.uk/explore-mental-health/publications/taking-sleep-seriously) makes the case that sleep belongs at the centre of mental health strategies.

**Try this:**

- Keep a regular sleep and wake time.
- Put your phone on silent or do-not-disturb at night, as [Mind's sleep tips](https://www.mind.org.uk/information-support/types-of-mental-health-problems/sleep-problems/tips-to-improve-your-sleep/) suggest.
- If sleep problems persist, speak to a doctor. [Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I)](https://www.mind.org.uk/information-support/types-of-mental-health-problems/sleep-problems/tips-to-improve-your-sleep/) is a recognised treatment.
- The NHS's [Every Mind Matters sleep page](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-health-issues/sleep/) is a good place to start.

### 4. Give, and keep learning

Small acts of kindness are good for the giver too. The NHS notes that [giving can create positive feelings, a sense of purpose and stronger connections](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/five-steps-to-mental-wellbeing). That could be a thank-you, a compliment or volunteering. [Learning new skills](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stress-anxiety-depression/improve-mental-wellbeing/) can boost confidence, build a sense of purpose and help you meet people.

**Try this:** Do one deliberate kind thing this week. Then pick a small skill to learn, like a recipe, an instrument, a language or fixing something around the house. It doesn't need to be impressive, just new and engaging.

### 5. Practise being present

Mindfulness means paying attention to the present moment, including your thoughts, feelings, body and surroundings. A large [Cambridge-led review in *PLOS Medicine*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7799763) pooled 136 trials with over 11,000 participants. Compared with doing nothing, mindfulness programmes reduced anxiety, depression and stress in most community settings, but not all.

The researchers were also candid that [mindfulness wasn't better than other "feel-good" practices](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/mindfulness-can-improve-mental-health-and-wellbeing-but-unlikely-to-work-for-everyone) such as exercise, and that it won't suit everyone. If it doesn't work for you, that's fine. Try something else from this list.

**Try this:** Spend five minutes noticing your breathing or your surroundings without judging what you find. When your mind wanders, gently bring it back.

### Putting it together

You don't have to do everything at once. Pick one habit and make it easy to repeat. The five steps reinforce each other too. A walk with a friend covers connection, movement and presence in one go.

These habits support wellbeing, but they aren't a substitute for professional help. If low mood, anxiety or sleep problems are lasting or getting in the way of daily life, please talk to a doctor or a mental health professional. Reaching out is a strength, not a failure.
