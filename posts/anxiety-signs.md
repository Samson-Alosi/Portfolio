Anxiety often shows up before we fully name it. It may begin as a restless feeling, an uneasy stomach, racing thoughts, or a strong urge to avoid something. Sometimes we do not realize we are anxious until we feel unusually tense, tired, or overwhelmed.

The first step is noticing the early signs. Anxiety can appear physically, mentally, and emotionally. You might notice tension in your shoulders, shallow breathing, difficulty concentrating, or a sense that something feels "off" even when there is no obvious danger. These signs are not signs of weakness. They are signal lights that your mind and body need support.

When you catch anxiety early, you have more room to respond before it grows bigger. A simple strategy is to pause and ask yourself: "What am I feeling right now?" Then name the sensation without judging it. Are you restless? Overwhelmed? Alert? Fearful? This kind of awareness helps you stop treating anxiety like an enemy and begin seeing it as information.

A few simple tools can help:

- Breathe slowly for a minute: inhale for four counts and exhale for six counts.
- Notice three things you can see around you to bring your attention back into the present.
- Put your attention on one task at a time instead of letting your mind spiral into future worries.
- Write down the most intense thought you are having and ask: "What evidence supports this? What evidence does not?"

Anxiety tends to grow when we stay in our thoughts without grounding ourselves. Slowing down and noticing what is happening gives us a more accurate picture. This is not about eliminating anxiety completely; it is about learning to respond with calm and clarity.

If anxiety is becoming frequent or intense, reaching out for support can be a wise step. You do not have to wait until things feel unbearable before asking for help. Seeking support is a sign of self-awareness, not failure.
